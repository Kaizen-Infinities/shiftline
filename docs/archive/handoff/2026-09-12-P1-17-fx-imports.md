# P1-17 — Foreign exchange and imports

## Shipped

- **Rate providers** behind an `ExchangeRateProvider` interface: a fixture adapter with a
  deterministic wobble (so a gain-and-loss test has something to work with) and an RBI reference
  adapter. A daily pg-boss job (14:00 IST, after the RBI publishes around 13:30) pulls for every
  tenant, and the pull result names the currencies that came back with **nothing** — a holiday has
  no RBI rate at all, and a caller that treats the gap as zero puts a zero rate into the books.
- **Realised gain and loss** (`fxService.realise`): the difference between the rate a document was
  raised at and the rate the money came in at, posted on the settlement date, with the party
  account carrying the correction so its balance still agrees with what is owed. Idempotent on the
  document, and a difference under a paisa posts nothing.
- **Unrealised restatement** (`fxService.revalue`): every open foreign-currency balance restated
  at the closing rate, posted on the last day of the period **with its reversal on the first day
  of the next, in the same transaction**. A period can be restated once.
- **Imports**: `BillOfEntry` with assessable value, basic duty, social welfare surcharge, IGST,
  cess and clearing charges, posted so that the non-creditable duty goes into the cost of the
  goods and the IGST goes to the input-credit ledger. `landedCostBreakdown` apportions the landed
  cost across the purchase's lines **by value**, with the rounding difference put on the largest
  line rather than lost.
- Three seed ledgers (`FX-GAIN-001`, `FX-LOSS-001`, `CUSTOMS-PAY-001`), permissions
  `imports.{view,create,manage}` granted to Owner and Accounts, migration
  `20260912200000_fx_imports` with RLS and audit triggers, web `/accounts/forex` with exposure,
  restatement and imports, in `e2e/smoke.spec.ts`. `fx.spec.ts` (9 tests).

## Decisions worth knowing

- **The reversal is created in the same transaction as the restatement.** It is impossible to
  have one without the other. Leaving an unrealised difference standing is the mistake that
  silently doubles a year's exchange gain: the movement is counted once at the period end and
  again when the money actually arrives. The test asserts the two vouchers are mirror images on
  consecutive days.
- **Realised and unrealised are different things and are posted differently.** One is a fact on a
  date; the other is an opinion about a rate. The screen says so in as many words, because an
  accountant who does not know the entry reverses will double-count it by hand.
- **IGST on an import never reaches the cost of the goods.** Putting the whole customs bill into
  stock overstates it by the IGST and throws the credit away. The split is the reason the module
  exists and the integration test checks all three ledgers.
- **Landed cost is apportioned by value, not by quantity.** By quantity, a cheap heavy item
  carries the duty of an expensive light one.
- **The customs exchange rate is not the market rate.** It is notified fortnightly, so it is
  stored on the bill of entry rather than looked up — and the dialog says so.
- **The RBI provider returns nothing when no feed is configured**, rather than inventing a rate.
  A made-up rate flows straight into the books.

## Half-done / known gaps

- **Exposure covers sales invoices only.** Purchase bills have no currency column yet (P1-14
  added it to `SalesInvoice` alone), so an importer's payables are not restated. The exposure
  builder is written to take more document types; the schema has to catch up first.
- Nothing calls `realise` automatically. Recording a receipt against a foreign-currency invoice
  does not yet ask for the rate it came in at, so the realised difference is posted through the
  API by hand. Wiring it into the receipt path is the obvious next step and belongs with
  whichever screen records the settlement.
- The restatement posts one net entry to Sundry Debtors rather than one per party. That is right
  in total and wrong per customer; a party-wise restatement needs the party ledger on each line
  and is worth doing before an audit.
- No forward contracts, no hedge accounting, no second reporting currency.
- The bill of entry does not update the purchase's stock valuation — `landedCostBreakdown` says
  what each line cost, but writing it back into inventory belongs with P1-19, where the valuation
  is being built.
- Duty drawback and advance authorisation are not modelled.

## Changelog sentence

Foreign-currency balances now carry their exchange difference properly — the realised gain or
loss when a bill is settled, a period-end restatement that reverses itself the next day, and
imports split so that customs duty lands in the cost of the goods while the IGST stays creditable.
