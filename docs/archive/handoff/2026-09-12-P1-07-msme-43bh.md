# P1-07 — MSME dues and section 43B(h)

## Shipped

- MSME status on the supplier: category (micro, small, medium, not registered), Udyam number
  validated against `UDYAM-XX-00-0000000`, the date the certificate was seen, whether there is a
  written contract, and the agreed payment days — with the fields on the supplier form.
- The section 15 clock per bill (`msme.service.ts`), counted from the **day of acceptance**
  (receipt date, falling back to the bill date), with the limit **derived**: 15 days without a
  written contract, the agreed days capped at 45 with one.
- `GET /api/msme/bills`: every bill from a micro or small supplier with what is outstanding, when
  it is due, how many days are left, whether it is late, and whether it was paid late.
- `GET /api/msme/disallowance/:fyCode`: section 43B(h) as at a year end, in two halves —
  **disallowed** (unpaid at the year end and past the section 15 date, out of this year's profit)
  and **allowed on payment** (paid late during the year, so the deduction comes back now) —
  each with the bills and a per-supplier total.
- `GET /api/msme/form1/:periodKey` and its `/register`: MSME Form 1, the half-yearly MCA return
  of dues beyond 45 days, on the filing gate as `MSME_1` (due 31 October and 30 April, wired into
  the compliance calendar).
- Daily pg-boss job `accounts.msme.clock` (08:15 IST) nudging at 15 and 2 days left — day 30 and
  day 43 on a 45-day clock — de-duplicated on the notification link.
- Web `/payable/msme` with the clock, the year-end adjustment and Form 1, in `e2e/smoke.spec.ts`.
  `msme.spec.ts` (7 tests). Migration `20260912160000_msme_43bh` — columns on an already-audited,
  already-RLS'd table, so no new policy block.

## Decisions worth knowing

- **The limit is derived, never typed.** A supplier who agreed to 60 days is recorded as agreeing
  to 60 — that is the truth of the contract — but the clock still runs at 45, because section 15
  caps it. Storing 60 as the limit would have made the disallowance report agree with the
  contract and disagree with the Act.
- **Acceptance, not invoicing.** Section 15 counts from the day the goods were accepted, which is
  often days after the supplier's invoice date. Using the invoice date would have made every
  clock optimistic by exactly the length of the delivery.
- **Two numbers at the year end, not one.** An accountant who is only told what is disallowed
  makes half the adjustment: last year's disallowance comes back as a deduction the year it is
  paid, and that number is on the same screen.
- **Form 1 uses the statutory 45 days, not the supplier's own limit.** The MCA return asks for
  dues beyond 45 days whatever was agreed, so a bill on a 15-day clock is late for section 15
  without yet being reportable. The two thresholds are deliberately different.
- **Medium enterprises are out.** 43B(h) reaches micro and small only, and a report that included
  medium suppliers would overstate the disallowance — tested directly.

## Half-done / known gaps

- Only GRN invoices carry the clock. Expenses booked against a supplier (`Expense.supplierId`) do
  not, and a service bill from a small vendor is just as much within section 15. The report reads
  one table; adding expenses is a second source in `bills()`.
- Interest under section 16 (three times the bank rate, compounded monthly) is not computed or
  posted. It is real and almost never paid; posting it would misstate the books, so it stays out
  until someone asks.
- The Udyam number is validated for shape only. Verifying it against the government portal needs
  a paid API.
- Nothing stops a payment run from paying a large supplier before a small one that is about to
  run out of time. The data to order it that way is here; the payment screens do not use it yet.
- The disallowance is a report, not a journal. No provision is posted and the tax computation is
  outside the product.
- A partly-paid bill is disallowed for the whole balance outstanding. That is the right reading —
  the unpaid part is what is disallowed — but a bill paid in instalments across the year end has
  its late instalments reported only through the balance, not instalment by instalment.

## Blocked on you

- Whether suppliers' Udyam certificates have been collected. The category defaults to "not
  registered", which means a company that has not filled this in sees an empty report and a clean
  bill of health it has not earned.

## Changelog sentence

Micro and small suppliers now carry the MSMED Act's payment clock — 15 days, or 45 with a written
agreement — with warnings before it runs out, a section 43B(h) report showing what is disallowed
this year and what comes back, and the half-yearly MSME Form 1.
